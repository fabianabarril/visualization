    This dashboard presents business performance metrics for a coffee shop. It is an effective example of good data visualization because it meets the aesthetic, substantive, and perceptual qualities introduced in our Visualization course.

    The dashboard is visually pleasing and well-organized (aesthetic). It uses a consistent colour palette, legible typography, and a balanced layout. These design choices make the visualization easy to interpret without unnecessary visual noise. It appears to accurately represent sales and operational data, integrating multiple views (bar charts, line charts) to show performance trends and comparisons across categories. BAD VISUALIZATION:
    title: “1 dataset, 100 visualizations – #14” (Data Viz Project)
    url: (https://100.datavizproject.com/data-type/viz14/)

    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
      ```
    This visualization is part of a design project where one dataset is shown in 100 different ways. In this version, the designer used radial donut charts to show information but it is not a good way to communicate the data clearly, as it should be aesthetic, substantive, and perceptual. This example fails in most of these areas.
    The chart makes it hard to understand what the numbers really mean. The viewer can see two circular shapes, but it is not clear how to read the values or compare the countries. It also does not say if the values represent counts, percentages, or something else. This goes against the idea from the course that data must be shown honestly and in context. Because the chart hides the real scale and uses design tricks like curved lines, it can easily mislead the viewer. 
    When values are placed around a circle, it becomes difficult to see which one is larger. The 2004 and 2022 rings use different densities of lines, which makes it look like there was a big increase even if the real difference might be small. The legend and colours do not make the story clear. As discussed in the course, this fails the perceptual quality — we cannot easily understand the main message or compare the data correctly.
    Even though the design looks modern, it focuses too much on being “cool” instead of being useful. The visualization seems made to show design skills, not data clarity. Tufte’s idea of keeping a high data-ink ratio is ignored here — a lot of the space is decoration instead of information. It is visually busy and not accessible for readers with colour-vision problems.

      ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
      To make this visualization better, the designer could replace the circular format with a simple bar chart that compares the three countries side by side for each year. Adding clear labels, axes, and annotations would help the reader see the changes more easily. The colours should have enough contrast and be explained in a visible legend. Finally, a short subtitle could tell viewers what the main message is.
      This example shows that a creative design is not always a good visualization. When the focus is on style instead of clear communication, the audience loses the message — which goes against what this course teaches about good data visualization.
      
      ```
